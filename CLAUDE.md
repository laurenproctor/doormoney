# Door Money

Door Money is a category-extensible, city-agnostic sponsorship marketplace connecting sponsors with relevant audiences. We start with music, sports teams, film, theater, and restaurants and hospitality, with Other for a project that fits none of them, and intend to expand to as many categories as can support a clear, deliverable sponsorship promise. Organizers raise money by offering specified visibility. Every sponsorship must make clear both what the funding enables and what the sponsor can count on receiving.

Two of the six joined the first four on 2026-09-21, and both are draft-only. **Restaurants & hospitality** (key `hospitality`: restaurants, bars, hotels, caterers, community kitchens) lets an organizer save a private draft and price options on it, and it cannot be published, found or paid for. **Other** (key `other`) is for an organizer whose work fits no named category, so that nobody is pushed into the nearest wrong one. It saves a private draft and nothing more, and has no templates, prices or starter kits. See "Categories, and what each may do" below before touching either.

New York may supply more early testers because of the founder's network; it is not an eligibility boundary or a technical default. Design for organizers and audiences anywhere, including online and multi-location activity. Category and geography must not be fixed to the initial cohort. Actual payment availability must reflect supported capabilities, not imply that every country and currency is already enabled.

Read `docs/PRODUCT_CONTRACT.md` first for the current product contract, category definitions, and compatibility rules. `docs/EXPANSION_PLAN.md` governs the five expansion phases. Decision 17 in `docs/DECISIONS.md` supersedes the earlier music-only product scope. `docs/ROADMAP.md` records the original music build, not the expansion plan.

## Vocabulary

Shared language describes the exchange; category language describes the work. Decision 17 supersedes decision 14's fixed music identity while preserving its plain-language and address-compatibility rules.

- **Organizer**: the person or organization responsible for a fundraiser and its promised delivery. Use this on shared creation and account surfaces. Use musician, team, filmmaker, theater company, or hospitality venue where the category is known; the words come from `organizerNoun` in `src/lib/categories.ts`, and a category it has no words for gets "organizer". A category is not an account role, and an account may organize and sponsor.
- **Sponsor**: someone buying specified visibility through a sponsorship. Use this throughout discovery, offers, checkout, delivery, and the transaction record.
- **Patron**: the broader relationship of supporting work or a community. Use patron rather than supporter as the generic account label. Existing patron accounts and addresses remain valid. Patronage can express purpose; it must not obscure what a sponsor receives.
- **Backer / backing**: the existing recognition contribution through the widget. Keep it distinct from a sponsorship. Do not relabel historic backings as sponsorships or promise new benefits on them.
- **Fundraiser**: one named funding effort with its own category, purpose, goal, timeline, and sponsorship options. Use the actual period where useful: tour, season, production, screening series, dinner series, or residency. Never board, campaign, or run as the generic product noun.
- **Sponsorship option**: a priced offer describing the visibility available. **Sponsorship** is the exchange. Fixed price and bidding are sale methods, not different categories.
- **Placement**: where a sponsor's name, logo, product, or message appears. It may be physical, digital, printed, or part of a production. The complete purchase is a sponsorship.
- **Deliverable**: a specific promised action or appearance, with a due date or delivery window. **Evidence** documents that delivery; it is not a Door Money certification.
- **Logo**: a sponsor's name or logo for an offer that needs it. Some offers need other materials; logo approval must not become the universal definition of delivery.
- **Record**: the sponsor's summary of the purchased offer, delivery, evidence, and payment outcomes. Keep Stripe receipts distinct from this record.
- **Profile**: a page about an organizer or patron; name the kind when ambiguous. A patron profile may be for a person or an organization (`profile_kind`, optional). The field once labeled "Music preferences" is labeled **Interests**; the stored values are kept exactly as typed and are never read as categories. The categories a patron supports are a separate answer (`patron_profile_categories`, migration 0043), so "Jazz" typed in 2026 is not a vote for music.
- **Suggested prices**: template defaults. The organizer's chosen price wins.

Door Money leads with relevant audiences, specified visibility, and meaningful support. It does not guarantee sales, audience growth, impressions, distribution, or outcomes it cannot substantiate. Sponsorship is the product's exchange; patronage expresses why that exchange can matter.

Addresses outlive words. Preserve `acts`, `runs`, `lots`, `patrons`, `mark_*`, sent-email links, and existing music addresses. The neutral `organizer` role exists (migration 0038) and the `musician` stored role is still read everywhere a role is checked: never drop it from a row or a check. The fundraisers index is at `/fundraisers`. `/auctions` was its first address and is in sent email and pasted widget snippets, so it redirects there (307, in `next.config.ts`) and must keep doing so; `/fundraiser` redirects too. All three words stay reserved in both namespace guards. Link to `/fundraisers` from inside the site: a test fails on any link to the old address. `/api/cron/auctions` and `src/lib/auctions.ts` keep the word on purpose. The first is called by the database from a URL held in Vault (migration 0036) and is not a page; the second is about bidding. Never use a global search-and-replace to rename these identifiers.

`tests/vocabulary.test.ts` guards the current app's retired wording and the authoritative contract's broader scope. Category-specific music wording stays valid. Shared pages, dashboard copy, templates and email are category-aware now, and `tests/category-neutral-copy.test.ts` holds the behavior to it: the words a category's helpers choose, the site strings, the email, the addresses. A phrase goes on a shared surface only if it is true of every category. The phrase sweep over page source is `tests/advisory/copy-sweep.test.ts`, run by `npm run test:copy` after a copy pass and never by CI: it is a heuristic, single-word labels and interpolated strings still require manual review, and a failure is a question for a person, not a verdict. The four legal pages retain their existing review deferral; launch requires their terms to match the implemented policies.

## Voice rules for anything user-facing

These are firm. They apply to page copy, button labels, emails, error messages, empty states and placeholder text.

1. **The second person only where "you" can mean one person.** The site serves organizers and sponsors, usually on the same page, so editorial copy stays in the third person and names the side it means: "Organizers set their own prices," not "you set your prices." That covers home, how sponsorship works, the fundraiser index, an act's page, a fundraiser's page and the legal pages. Transactional surfaces go the other way: sign up, sign in, the password flows, the dashboard, the patron pages behind an account, form labels, helper text, validation messages, the record and any email about somebody's own money are written to the person doing the thing, in the second person. A marketing page with only one audience (the organizer invitation, the widget) may use either, and both use the second person today. Button labels are imperative or nominal everywhere: "Create a fundraiser", "Find sponsorships", "Create an account". Settled in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, decision 15.
2. **Active voice.** Name who does what. "Door Money holds the money," not "the money is held." "Patrons put the money up," not "the money is put up."
3. **Benefits before mechanics.** Lead with what sponsorship funds for the organizer (travel, equipment, production, or performances) and what a sponsor gets (attention, a name in the room, support they can point at). Mechanics (holds, weekly payouts, approvals) come second and stay short.
4. **Plain words.** No insider phrasing, no jargon, no cleverness that needs decoding. "When Door Money opens," not "when doors open." "Attendance," not "through the door."
5. **No em dashes.** Anywhere. Use a comma, a colon, a period, or parentheses.
6. **No invented proof.** A fundraiser says what its organizer actually committed to, and nothing more. The verification methods live in `src/lib/verification.ts` and reach the page through `PlacementVerification`; never write proof language by hand into a page. Don't promise documentation from every show: the methods say "selected shows" because that is the promise. Don't imply Door Money inspected anything, so no "verified by Door Money", no "confirmed", no certification language. Documentation comes from the organizer and Door Money passes it on. `verificationMethods(categoryKey)` rewords the list for sports, film, theater and hospitality. A category with no rewording is shown music's sentences ("selected shows"), so a new category needs words there as well as in `src/lib/categories.ts`; `tests/verification.test.ts` reads the registry from the migrations and fails until it has them. Hospitality's say to photograph the placement, not the guests. Evidence itself is category-aware (see the delivery rules below). Keep the commitment the size it is (see `docs/DECISIONS.md`, decision 9).
7. **Short sentences.** Cut the second clause when the first one already lands.

## Design system

Tokens live in `src/app/globals.css`. Use them; don't introduce new colors or fonts.

Every page is a dark room with one color of light in it. The room is the same on every page; the light changes per page. Three stage lights (`StageLights`, mounted by `Theme`) throw the accent down the room from a truss above the page and swing as the reader scrolls; they hold still under reduced motion.

**Two rooms, one brand.** A reader may turn the house lights up: `data-mode="light"` on `<html>`, set by `ModeToggle` in the nav and kept in that browser alone (`src/lib/mode.ts`, no cookie, no account, nothing sent anywhere). Dark is the default and the absence of the attribute, so the markup a first visitor is served carries none and a browser with no script gets the room as it was built. The owner overturned the earlier "no light mode" rule on 2026-09-22; dark stays the default and there is no third mode.

The color of light never changes with the room. Each theme keeps its `accent` hex in both, because that is the page's color and a second set would be a second brand. What changes is the room around it: `ground`, `ink`, `line`, `field-line`, `panel`, and the two tokens that describe how light falls on a lifted block, `glint` and `pool`. `accent-ink` changes too and has to: it is the tint of the accent that clears 4.5:1 on its ground, and on a near-white ground that is a *darker* tint. The light values are computed against their own grounds, never picked, and they are aimed past the bar rather than at it: accent-ink and muted both at 7:1, the AAA bar for body text. The headroom is not decoration. Both also have to read inside a hero, where they sit on a wash of the accent rather than on the bare ground and give up about a fifth of their contrast; on that wash the tightest are 5.79:1 and 5.25:1. Ink clears 17.7:1. `tests/contrast.test.ts` measures all of it out of `globals.css`, in both rooms, and is the reason a hand-picked hex cannot get in. Move a ground and every value above it has to be computed again. `field-line` is also its own number in each room (0.42 dark, 0.47 light): a control boundary wants 3:1, and near-black over near-white is a shallower step than cream over the dark ground, so the dark room's alpha measures only 2.71:1 here, against 3.15:1 at 0.47. Mono is the one theme that turns over: its light is the ink itself, so the cream becomes the ground and the near-black becomes the accent.

- The room: `ground` (page background), `ink` `#F4F0E8` (text), `muted` (secondary text), `line` (1px rules and borders), `panel` (a lifted, translucent block).
- The light: `accent` (fills, glows, the bloom: anything whose job is to be the color), `accent-ink` (the tint of the accent that can be read as text; use it for any accent text), `accent-line` (the edge of something a person has to find and operate, which WCAG asks to clear 3:1), `on-accent` (text on an accent fill).
- Three cuts of one accent, and they are not interchangeable. `accent-line` is the accent in the dark room and the readable tint in the light one, because lime measures 1.16:1 on its own near-white ground, amber 1.77 and teal 1.59: a focus ring, a button's edge or a selected tile drawn in the raw accent is simply not there. Never write `border-accent` or an accent focus ring on anything interactive; that is what `accent-line` is for. It is declared on every themed element, never once on `:root`, because a custom property is substituted where it is declared and would otherwise stay the house blue under every other light.
- The H1 is set one step heavier in the light room (`[data-mode="light"] .display`). Bodoni is a didone whose thin strokes are very thin, dark type on paper reads lighter than light type on a dark ground, and a ratio does not capture it: a hairline at 7:1 still reads faint. Same size, same words; the compensation is optical.
- Themes, set with `<Theme name>` or the `theme` prop on `Page`: blue (home, sign in, sign up, dashboard, the embed), lime (how sponsorship works, and music's own page under it), magenta (the fundraisers index at `/fundraisers`), amber (create a fundraiser, at `/list`), teal (widget), violet (contact), red (404), mono (the legal pages). An organizer's pages take a color by slug through `themeFor`, so each organizer keeps the same light, whatever the category. A patron's public page takes the light the patron chose from `PROFILE_THEMES` in `src/lib/profile.ts` (blue when they never chose). That list is the colored themes and nothing else: never a typed color, and never mono.
- Smallest text on the site is 14px. Metadata and captions use 14 or 14.5px, body copy 15px and up.
- Bodoni Moda for H1s only, set in caps (the `display` utility; the accent word in a headline is italic). Archivo for everything else: headings below the H1 (the `heading` utility, medium weight), body, and tracked caps labels (the `caps` utility). Nothing else. On the Desk register the H1 is 36px and is the thing the reader owns, one per screen: the organizer's name on Today, the fundraiser's name on its own page. Caps there are for a status word inside a badge and nothing else, so the navigation, the buttons, a table's headers and every label are sentence case.
- Thin 1px lines (`edge`), no hard shadows, no tilt. No rounded corners on the Stage register except circles; on the Desk register cards are 8px (`--radius-card`), controls 6px (`--radius-control`) and pills are round. Blocks that should catch the light use `glow` or `lit`. Heroes carry a stage light (`HeroArt`); a photo dropped at `public/hero/<theme>.jpg` appears under it, or pass `photo` to name the file (the home page uses `hero/saxophone.jpg`). In the light room the photograph keeps the right of the hero and stays out from under the words, and on a phone it is left out of the hero entirely. That is not a taste call: small text in the light room is a dark tint, and no combination of brightness, contrast and opacity lifts a photograph far enough for a dark tint to clear 4.5:1 on its shadows without erasing the picture. Never move a photograph back under a headline in that room. The other themes carry public domain Gottlieb club photographs; credits in `public/hero/CREDITS.md`.

### Two registers: Stage and Desk

**Stage** is the public site, exactly as it is. **Desk** is the signed-in workspace: the same room, the same light per organizer, scanned rather than read. The register is `data-register="desk"` on the workspace shell, a block of CSS scoped to it at the top of `globals.css`, and the primitives in `src/components/desk/`. `docs/DESK_REGISTER.md` is the build plan; decision 20 in `docs/DECISIONS.md` says what each register is for.

- Status has three states and nothing else on the register is colored. **ok** is the organizer's own light (`--ok`, `--ok-ink`, `--ok-wash`), so an open fundraiser under a blue organizer is blue and under a lime one is lime. **attention** is amber in every room and under every theme (`--attention`, `--attention-ink`, `--attention-wash`), which is what lets it mean one thing everywhere. **neutral** is the ink at low strength (`--neutral-ink`, `--neutral-wash`). The word carries the state; the dot beside it is `aria-hidden`, so nothing is said in color alone.
- `--surface` is what a workspace block is painted on and `--shadow-1` is the hint of a shadow under it. Both change with the room and neither changes with the light. `tests/contrast.test.ts` measures every status pair on its own pill, in both rooms, for all eight themes. Blue is the one theme that had to state a darker `--ok-ink` in the light room, and the file says why. When a pair fails, darken the ink; never lighten the ground, because a wash pale enough to rescue a tint is a status nobody notices.
- Inside `[data-register="desk"]` the stage lights, `hero-in` and `data-reveal` do not run. `<Theme lights={false}>` is how a page stops mounting them at all, and `.pool-static` is the one still pool of the accent at the top of the page that stands in for them.
- The primitives are `Card`, `Kpi`, `Badge`, `Table`, `TaskRow`, `MoneyBar`, `Tabs` and `RunStrip`, with `Button register="desk"` for every control (`solid`, `outline`, `quiet`, and `size="sm"` for a row's own action). There is one button in the codebase and it knows both registers; never write a second. They take plain props and reach nothing: no Supabase, no server action, no payment, no category. `tests/desk-components.test.ts` renders every one of them against the four fixtures and reads that boundary out of their source, along with the two rules this register breaks most easily: no text under 14px, and no color that is not a token. `RunStrip` is music's, the way `ShowsPanel` and `RunForm` are.
- Every page behind a sign-in is on the register, and each takes the light of what it is about: an organizer's pages take `themeFor(act.slug)` (Today, the fundraiser, Money), `/patron` takes the patron's own `PROFILE_THEMES` choice, `/dashboard/profile` and `/dashboard/account` are blue, and `/admin` is mono and passes `adminNav()` so staff are not offered an organizer's destinations.
- The top bar's search filters the page and finds a fundraiser, and asks the server nothing (`WorkspaceSearch`). A row it can filter carries `data-search` with its own words in it; the `Table` and `TaskRow` primitives write the attribute from a `search` prop, and `[data-register="desk"] [data-search][hidden]` in `globals.css` is what makes hiding one stick over a grid row's own display. Jumping to a fundraiser by title reads a list the page already had (Today, Money) or the one small read in `loadFundraiserJumps`. Cmd+K puts the cursor in the box. A page that passes no `search` prop gets no box, because a search that cannot find anything is worse than none.
- Money says the same thing everywhere it appears: money taken is the number, and a bid is named beside it and never added to it, because a bid is a hold on a card until an option closes. `HomeSponsorship.raisedCents` is every sponsorship and every backing that was charged, refunds off, which is the sum `src/lib/dashboard.ts` reports for one fundraiser.

## Engineering rules

Rules that are music's say so. Friday calendar transfers and logo approval are music's mechanics, not promises any other category makes. Expansion requirements live in the product contract.

- Next.js 16 App Router. `params` and `searchParams` are Promises: await them. `middleware.ts` is now `proxy.ts`.
- A signed-out visitor to `/dashboard` or `/admin` is redirected by `src/proxy.ts`, before any page runs, so a page's own `requireUser(next)` is never what they meet. `signInPath` in `src/lib/urls.ts` keeps the whole address, query string included, and `safeNext` decides whether it is a path inside the site. Test a redirect by requesting it, not by reading the page.
- Two-factor authentication is Supabase Auth's own TOTP factor and nothing else: an authenticator app, no SMS, no paid provider, and no secret in Door Money's database. `src/lib/mfa.ts` answers the two questions (does this account have a verified factor, and is this session at `aal2`), and both `requireUser` and `src/proxy.ts` send a session that owes its code to `/login/verify`. Every factor call runs on the visitor's own session through `supabaseServer()`: the service-role client must never enroll or verify a factor. An account may hold two apps (`TOTP_FACTOR_NAMES`), which is the recovery plan that needs no stored secret; recovery codes are the Supabase API that is still `@experimental` in auth-js 2.116.0, are enabled by a flag on the server client, and every call treats a refusal as "this project does not have them". The one service-role exception is `removeAccountTotp` on `/admin`, which only ever deletes a factor for somebody locked out. `/reset` is deliberately exempt so a password reset stays reachable; it does not remove the factor.
- The app must run with no database at all (README, "Run it"). `supabaseServer()` throws when unconfigured, so a public page that asks who is signed in guards for it, the way `/list` does.
- Server components by default. Add `"use client"` only for interactivity.
- Supabase for Postgres, Auth, Realtime and Storage. Server-side client in `src/lib/supabase/server.ts`, browser client in `src/lib/supabase/client.ts`. Never use the service-role key in client code.
- Stripe Connect with Express accounts, separate charges and transfers. The patron pays Door Money through an embedded Checkout Session; the charge sits on the platform balance and Transfers (with `source_transaction`) move the organizer's share out: weekly across the fundraiser's dates for music, and one documented deliverable at a time everywhere else (next rule). Door Money's 15% is the part never transferred: the schedule is built from amount minus `fee_cents`. Never `application_fee_amount`, never `transfer_data` on the charge. Every Stripe webhook handler must be idempotent: check `stripe_events` before acting, and make each write conditional on the state it expects.
- Delivery and release follow `docs/DELIVERY_POLICY_MATRIX.md`, held in `delivery_policies` by category and version (migration 0045). Music is version 1 on the **calendar** rule and is exactly what it was. Every other category is on the **evidence** rule: nothing is scheduled at payment, and the organizer's share is laid one deliverable at a time as the organizer documents it (`src/lib/delivery.ts`), through the same Friday job, the same 0031 materials gate and the same idempotency key. `purchases.mark_status` means "the organizer accepted the sponsor's materials", whatever they are. Never call it a logo outside music.
- A purchase's `purchase_snapshots` row is what was bought and the policy version it was bought under. A trigger writes it once and nothing may edit it. Read a purchase's promise from there, never from the live fundraiser, profile, template or price.
- Evidence is private by default, item by item. A public fundraiser publishes none of it. The public reads `public_evidence` and nothing else; a page that anybody can open (the record) reads private evidence under the visitor's own session so row level security decides, never with the service role. An item showing a minor, and anything from a youth team, is never publishable.
- A policy with status `proposed` is test mode only. Only the owner switches one to `active`, and a category with no policy row cannot be bought at all. `paymentsOpenFor` in `src/lib/payment-gate.ts` asks this before checkout or a bid reaches Stripe. Known gap: when the policy read itself fails, its fallback answers yes for any category but music in Stripe test mode, which is looser than "no policy row". The database still refuses the purchase (`no_delivery_policy`).
- Money is stored as integer cents in Postgres, never floats. Format with `formatMoney` from `src/lib/money.ts`.
- The widget at `/embed/[slug]` must be frameable by any origin; nothing else may be. See `next.config.ts` headers.
- `src/lib/catalog.ts` holds the words for every category's sponsorship options, held equal to the `surfaces` rows by `tests/catalog.test.ts`. Only music's carry a suggested price, because only music has sales history. A suggestion is a default; the organizer's own price on a lot always wins.
- Three things stay distinct: a **template** (`surfaces`, what Door Money suggests), an **opportunity** (`lots`, what one organizer chose to offer at their own price), and a **purchased offer** (`purchases`, at the lot's `offer_version`). `src/lib/opportunities.ts` is the adapter. A template promises nothing: never describe an option an organizer has not ticked as though it were offered, and never suggest a price for a category Door Money has no sales history in.
- The `surfaces` table is the template registry, read through `loadTemplates`. `src/lib/catalog.ts` adds longer words for the options it knows and is never the list of what exists, so a new category is registry rows and tests, not a union type or a Postgres enum. Never loop over `CATALOG` to decide what a fundraiser may offer.
- A **starter kit** (`src/lib/starter-kits.ts`) is a fundraising situation: fund a tour, fund a season, fund a short film. It sits above the templates and is never one. It points at template keys, carries no price in any category, and creates no row of any kind. What it prefills are examples the organizer picked and can edit: a working name, the purpose, the audience, the sponsor promise, and the details choosing it makes true. Never a goal, a date, a count, a location or a number, and the example promise is never bigger than "where their sponsorship option says". The new-fundraiser form offers a kit or an empty start (`/dashboard/runs/new?template=<key>` links to one); its rules are the reducer in `src/lib/starter-kit-draft.ts`: a kit fills only empty fields, applies only to its own category, and switching category drops the kit, its unedited examples and the old category's details. The chosen kit is creation context, not data: `runs` has no column for it and the draft schema refuses it, so it lives in form state and as `?kit=` in the address after the first save. The registry decides what a kit may do: `starterKitAvailability` answers unavailable for a category with no `fundraiser_categories` row. The organizer page (`/list`) shows kits as sponsorship ideas from `src/lib/organizer-examples.ts`; an idea links into the form only where the registry has its category (`exampleStatus`), and otherwise reads "Coming soon" and links nowhere near it. The kit is carried through sign-up and the organizer-profile step as `?template=`, looked up each time and never stored. Hospitality kits are `draftOnly` and resolve to draft_only (see the hospitality rule below).
- **Categories, and what each may do.** The database decides all three columns and no list in code may: `fundraiser_categories.draft_enabled`, `fundraiser_categories.publish_enabled`, and the category's newest `delivery_policies` row. A key that is not in the registry fails validation. It never falls back to music.

It is for an organizer whose work fits no named category, so that nobody is pushed into the nearest wrong one. It is a controlled way in, not a way around the explanation: it is held to the same test as every category (what the funding enables, who the audience is, what the sponsor receives, and how delivery will be documented), and the organizer states all of it in the shared fields. It borrows nothing: no other category's templates, detail fields, verification wording or nouns. Its words are the neutral ones (`organizer`, `fundraiser`, `materials`, `sponsors`) and its one form note lives in `src/lib/categories.ts`. Its key is `hospitality` and its public name is **Restaurants & hospitality** (the registry's label, migration 0049). Opening it takes two separate acts by the owner, a policy row (which arrives `proposed`) and `publish_enabled`, plus the kits' `draftOnly` flag. Never add either in a migration that does something else. `supabase/tests/hospitality_draft_only_test.sql` holds all of it.
- Category is never geography. No category names a city, and Restaurants & hospitality is no more a New York category than music is.
- What kind of sponsorship a template is (cash, product or beverage, a service, a space, an event, a guest experience) is words in `src/lib/sponsorship-kinds.ts`, listed beside the template in `src/lib/catalog.ts`. Door Money moves money and nothing else: product or services a sponsor supplies are agreed between the organizer and the sponsor, and no page may describe them as something bought here.
- A draft's sponsorship options are as private as the draft (migration 0048). `lots` is readable once its fundraiser has left draft, through `run_has_left_draft`; the organizer reads their own through `owns_run`. Before 0048 the policy from 0001 was `using (true)`, so every draft's prices were readable with the anon key.
- An opportunity belongs to its fundraiser's category. `guard_lot_category` (migration 0044) refuses a lot whose template is in another category, for every role, and `saveLots` reads the category from the fundraiser the session owns, never from the form. `lots.template_snapshot` records what the organizer chose from; the trigger writes it and no client may read or write it. The purchased-offer snapshot (`purchase_snapshots`, migration 0045) is built from it.
- **Discovery is structured, and never inferred from prose.** `docs/DISCOVERY_CONTRACT.md` is the contract; migration 0053 is the schema. What a fundraiser is discoverable by is a key from a registry (`discovery_facets`, `discovery_tags`), never something read out of `purpose`, `description`, `audience_description` or `sponsor_promise`. Those four are unchanged and are still what a sponsor reads. A new facet is a row and a new tag is a row: there is no union type for either, and `src/lib/discovery.ts` stays pure. None of it is required to publish, so a fundraiser carrying no tags publishes exactly as it does today and a missing tag draws nothing, never a placeholder. Keep the six distinctions the contract lists apart, in particular a fundraiser's funding purpose from one sponsorship option, activity location (`runs.activity_locations`) from the organizer's own location (`acts.city`), and the fundraising end date (`runs.fundraising_ends_on`) from an option's closing time (`lots.closes_at`). `runs.activity_country_codes` is derived by the trigger from the locations and is in no write grant: never hand one in. A reach estimate (`lots.reach_estimate`) cannot be stored without its basis (`lots.reach_basis`), because an estimate with no basis is a claim. The public discovery surface is `public_fundraiser_discovery` and `public_opportunity_discovery` and nothing else: no table's `anon` grants were widened for it, and neither view carries a patron, a bid, an amount, a fee, a Stripe id, evidence or a delivery state. `/fundraisers` reads those two views and nothing else, in two queries whatever the number of fundraisers: never `listOpenBoards`, which loads a whole board per organizer, bids, buyers and backers included. An offer's close is the view's `effective_closes_at`, which is `lot_close_time`'s rule applied to bidding only; a fixed-price option is not on the fundraiser's bidding clock and the activity end date is never an offer's closing date. A price filter reads the organizer's own number, and a bidding option matches on its reserve or its take-it-now price. Most relevant is four published comparisons in a fixed order and is written out in `docs/DISCOVERY_CONTRACT.md`: never a model, never personalization, and never anything an organizer can buy.
- Validate every API input with zod. Return typed errors, never raw exceptions.
- Public reads of anything a patron owns go through a sanitised view (`public_patron_profiles`, `public_patron_activity`, `lot_buyers`, `run_backers`), granted to `anon` and selecting only public columns. Never open `profiles`, `purchases`, `backings`, `bids` or `patrons` to the browser: they hold email addresses, amounts and Stripe ids. Selecting a private column and hiding it in React is not privacy.
- A patron's public profile and each item on it are off by default, in the database, and are turned on one at a time. An anonymous bid is never publishable, whatever a form says.
- A view is a read path and never a write path. Every public view here is built `with (security_invoker = false)` so it can select past row level security, which also means a *write* through it reaches the base table with RLS switched off. Postgres will auto-update any view simple enough (one table, no join, no union), so the only thing standing between the default grants and a rewritten table is the view's shape. Revoke insert, update, delete and truncate on every view, and never grant them back. Migration 0030 closed this; `supabase/tests/permissions_test.sql` holds it.
- TRUNCATE ignores row level security by design, so a policy is no defence against it. `anon` and `authenticated` hold it on nothing.
- A new table is outside the Data API boundary until its grants are revoked. Row level security with no policy still answers PostgREST `200 []` rather than refusing, so the migration that creates a table decides its `anon` and `authenticated` grants in the same file, and `supabase/tests/permissions_test.sql` gets an assertion for it. Migration 0022 was a snapshot; 0029 had to catch up eight tables it never covered.
- A word in the namespace (`profiles.username` and `acts.slug`) is claimed through two database functions and nowhere else. `claim_username` (migration 0024) is the person's word: it holds the twelve-month rule and the retired-word list, and it moves an organizer's address with the handle only when the organizer was holding that same word, which is every organizer made before 0058. `claim_act_slug` (migration 0058) is the organizer's word: it creates the row or renames one holding a word of its own, and it never reads or writes `profiles.username`, so setting up or renaming an organization never changes what somebody signs in with. It answers `is_handle` for an organizer whose address is the handle, and `saveAct` then goes through `claim_username`, so decision 8's pair still moves together. Both look in the same three places and take the same advisory lock, so neither door can hand out the other's word. `supabase/tests/organizer_address_test.sql` holds all of it. `RESERVED_SLUGS` in `src/lib/slug.ts` still has to grow whenever a top-level route does.
- Patron profile photos live in the private `patron-photos` bucket and are only ever reached through a short-lived signed URL minted on the server. Act and show photos stay in their public buckets. A patron's header image lives in the same private bucket and is signed the same way; `HeroArt` draws it with `signed`, as a plain image, because the image optimizer only fetches public storage paths. The photo may be a GIF; the header may not.
- Which categories a patron may say they support is `fundraiser_categories.preference_enabled` (migration 0050), read by the profile form and checked again by the save. It is not `publish_enabled`: the two questions are different, and a new category starts off for both. The `other` category is never a preference. "Other" on the profile form is a tag the patron types (`patron_profiles.custom_tag`, `parseCustomTag`), stored as text and never read as a category, the same rule as Interests. No profile column is named for a category: `supabase/tests/neutral_profiles_test.sql` refuses one, because a category belongs to the fundraiser.
- Prefer server actions for form posts from our own pages; route handlers for webhooks and for anything the widget calls cross-origin.
- Door Money staff is one decision in one place: `requireAdmin` in `src/lib/admin.ts`, which asks `isAdminUser` whether the account Supabase's `auth.getUser()` reports has a confirmed email (`email_confirmed_at`) that is on `ADMIN_EMAILS`, compared trimmed and lower-cased. Every admin page and every admin server action calls it first, before reading any input, so an action refuses on its own. Never read `ADMIN_EMAILS` for an access decision anywhere else, and never decide from a session cookie's claims. Local `supabase/config.toml` has email confirmation off, which is why the listed address alone was enough before 2026-09-23; the hosted project keeps "Confirm email" on. `tests/admin.test.ts` holds the rule.

## Verifying a change

The owner asked on 2026-09-23 for less verification per change, so that building goes faster. The automated gates are cheap (the 647 unit tests run in 4 seconds, typecheck in 3, CI in about a minute and a half) and the time went into by-hand verification around them and into tests that needed editing on every PR. These are the rules now.

1. **Before a push: the tests that touch the change, and typecheck.** `node --test` on the affected files (`npm test -- tests/<file>.test.ts` also works) plus `npm run typecheck`. Do not run the full `npm run verify` locally: CI runs lint, the whole suite and the production build on every PR and push to main, and does it faster than this machine lints. A red CI run costs one more push; a green local run that CI repeats costs several minutes every time.
2. **The Docker database gate runs locally only when the migration touches what it tests.** That is grants, row level security, policies, views' security settings, triggers or a function that decides who may do what. A migration that adds a column, a row or a plain function gets its database run from CI, in 35 seconds. When it does run locally, `npm run test:db:docker` is the command, and Docker on this machine stalls under load, so check `docker ps` answers first.
3. **Browser verification is opt-in, and off by default.** The default proof of a change is rule 1, then CI, then the Vercel preview on the PR, which the owner opens. Stand up a local Supabase stack and a headless browser only when the change is a new signed-in screen with real layout, when the change is on the money path (checkout, bidding, a webhook, a payout), or when the owner asks. A PR that skipped it says so in one line ("Not seen in a browser") and says what the preview is the first look at. The owner is running a hands-on pass on production (see the memory note on the browser test pass), and a layout defect found there is cheap; a browser pass that finds nothing is not.
4. **One test per behavior, and no tripwires.** A new test holds a rule of the product or a bug that was found, and is written against the function or the action, not against the source text of a page. Never add a test that lists which files may mention a word, which migrations exist, or which phrases a page may contain: each one has to be edited on every PR that adds a file, and it has found nothing that the property it stands for would not. Where a rule is about the whole folder, write it as a property over the folder (the hospitality test, "no migration that names hospitality enables publishing") so a new file cannot fail it without breaking the rule. The gates worth their cost stay exactly as they are: `supabase/tests/permissions_test.sql` (it exists because migration 0022 reached production with a hole), the webhook idempotency and Stripe event tests, `tests/vocabulary.test.ts` and `tests/contrast.test.ts`, which are fast and computed. New tests are added when a behavior is new or a bug is fixed, and not to prove a PR was careful; when the owner says "no new tests", write none.
5. **A PR's verification section is one line.** What ran and what did not: "Typecheck and the six tests on the action pass; CI green; not seen in a browser." The reader is the owner, who merges by hand, and the section exists so the preview is opened with the right expectation, not to record effort.

Money and authentication keep the full treatment: a browser or Stripe test-mode pass before the PR, the database gate locally when a migration is involved, and the production HTTP checks after the merge. Everything else takes the lighter path above.

## Working with the mockups

`docs/mockups/*.html` are self-contained pages with inline CSS in the old paper look. They are the source for sections, order and copy, not for color or type. When porting one:
