Onstage

Every camera in the room finds these

A name on these surfaces rides along in every fan photo, every phone video and every review shot, night after night, for the price of one print run. Each format matches the band's own artwork, and every one needs the band's yes before it ships.

Kick drum head
from $1,200 a tour

The most photographed surface on any stage. Front and center in every wide shot, every phone video, every review photo. The patron's mark goes on as a stamp within the band's own drum head design, printed once for the run. One patron holds it at a time.

Seen by: everyone in the room, every night, plus everyone who watches the footage.
Road case spots
from $150 a spot

A sticker on the flight cases, designed to match the band's own artwork so it blends in. Cases sit stage-side all night and travel everywhere the band does. Four spots make the full stack.

Seen by: the room, load-ins, and every backstage photo.
Guitar straps
from $350 a run

Custom straps for the front line, with the patron's mark woven in. A strap crosses the player's chest in every front-on photo, and it looks like normal gear, because it is. The band keeps them after the run.

Seen by: every camera pointed at the stage.
Amp grilles
from $400 a pair

A mark on the amp cabinet grilles, stage left and right. Covers the side angles the kick drum head doesn't reach, and the amps are in every shot of the players.

Seen by: the room, side-stage photos, live video.
Riser fascia
from $200 a tour

The strip across the front of the drum riser. A small add-on that shows up in wide shots. Usually bought alongside the kick head, never instead of it.

Seen by: wide shots and video.

The room

The longest look a patron gets

The merch table and the tip jar hold a fan's attention longer than anything on the stage. People stop, read, and reach for a wallet, so a name in these spots earns more attention per dollar than any other surface a band owns. Residency placements live here too, priced monthly for house bands and resident acts.

Tip jar card
from $250 a month

The residency placement. A printed card on the tip jar of a house band: the patron's name, the band, one line. Every eye in the room finds the tip jar, every night the band plays. The most popular option for local businesses supporting a nearby venue.

Seen by: the whole room, weekly, including the patron's own regulars.
Stage thank-you
from $200 a month

Once a set, from the mic, the band names the patron keeping the night going. It's a live read, delivered by someone the room already trusts. Residency only, worded by the band.

Seen by: heard by everyone present.
Merch table runner
from $500 a tour

The patron's mark on the table where fans spend two to five minutes deciding. Includes a small counter card. The surface fans spend the most time in front of.

Seen by: every fan who buys or browses.
Hang tags
from $350 a tour

A tag on every piece of merch sold, the patron's name on the back of it. The only placement that goes home with the fan and stays there.

Seen by: every merch buyer, and everyone who sees them wear it.
Picks
from $200 a run

Branded picks, band on one face, the patron on the other. Played all night, thrown to the crowd at the end. Fans keep them, trade them, and post them.

Seen by: whoever catches one, and keeps it.

Online and in print

Exposure that comes with numbers

These placements carry their own numbers: post reach, email opens, video views. They travel past the room, and they make the easiest first buy for anyone who has never sponsored a band before.

Poster credit
from $300 a tour

A presented-by line on the tour poster and admat, everywhere the show is announced: venue walls, listings, the band's feed.

Seen by: everyone who sees the tour announced.
Posts and email
from $400 a run

A named thank-you in the tour announcement post and the band's mailing list. Written by the band in their own voice, which is why it works.

Seen by: the band's full following.
Vlog logo card
from $250 a run

A card in the band's tour vlog or recap reel. Rolls with the footage fans actually rewatch.

Seen by: everyone who streams the recap.
Rig rundown
from $400

An integration in the band's gear walkthrough video. Watched almost exclusively by people who buy gear, which is why gear brands rate it above anything on the stage.

Seen by: players and gear buyers, the deepest-intent audience here.

Bands price touring placements per tour, typically 15 to 25 shows, and residency placements per month. Longer runs and bigger venues scale per date, so nobody renegotiates.

Exclusivity is for sale too

Category exclusivity

By default a band can carry more than one patron. Being the only name in a category on a run, or the only patron on a whole tour, is a premium the band can choose to offer. Priced by the band, like everything else here.

Before signing

The rules that keep it worth buying

The rules that keep bands safe are the same ones that keep a placement worth having. A stage that doesn't look bought is the only kind worth being on.

x Nothing goes up without the band's yes.
x Patrons put the money up before the first show.
x Bands get paid every Friday. No chasing.
x Patrons pay nothing for a placement that never runs.
x No placements at weddings or private events.
