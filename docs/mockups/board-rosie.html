Rosie the Bassoonist

Fall season. September to December, New York City.

Rosie is a gigging musician in NYC: a working bassoonist moving between the rehearsals, services, chamber dates, and sessions that keep a freelance player employed, with a feed where the bassoon does the talking. She is the shape of Door Money's solo tier: one player, small surfaces, small money, and a few hundred dollars a season that makes freelancing add up. The bassoon itself is not for sale and never will be. The case, the stand, and the feed are.

Who backs a bassoonist

Small patrons with perfect aim

The pitch here isn't reach. A solo player's following is small, but it's made of working players, serious students, and the rooms she gigs in, and for the right buyer that's their whole target market gathered in one place, reachable nowhere else this directly. These are the kinds of buyers each spot is built for, with real examples of who that means:

If a bid wins

What happens after Sunday

  1. The winning patron puts the money up within 48 hours, or the spot goes to the next bid.
  2. Rosie approves the mark. Nothing goes up without her yes.
  3. She plays the season she was already playing.
  4. The money reaches her through the season rather than at the end of it.
  5. End of season: a record of every gig the placement ran at.
